    EVENT ID 42 · 1

    PC Won't Wake From Sleep: Finding the Cause With Events 1 and 42

    Kernel-Power 42 is the record of entering sleep, and Power-Troubleshooter 1 is the record that explains the cause of waking from sleep. Looking at the two events together lets you tell whether the PC woke by itself or whether a user operated it.

    These two events are not codes that confirm a power problem right away. Only after you compare the sleep time in 42, the Wake Source in 1 and whether the mouse or keyboard was actually used can you check sleep settings or peripheral devices.

    Values to check in the records: in Kernel-Power 42, the time the PC entered sleep and the reason for sleep; in Power-Troubleshooter 1, the device or scheduled task shown as the Wake Source; and the repeat interval, to compare whether the same device wakes it every time or whether it repeats only at certain times.

    When it appears

    • When the PC enters sleep
    • When the PC wakes from sleep

    Likely causes

    • A device or scheduled task shown as the Wake Source (the mouse, keyboard, network or a scheduled task)

    What to check, in order

    1. Compare with actual use — compare the time you went to bed with the Wake Source of 1 and check whether it was a normal input.
    2. Check the powercfg results — in an administrator Command Prompt, run powercfg /lastwake and powercfg /waketimers to see the device that woke the PC most recently and the scheduled tasks. If a scheduled task is shown as the one that woke it, also check Sleep > Allow wake timers in the advanced power options. This setting decides whether a scheduled task can wake a sleeping PC, and the choices are "Disable", "Enable" and "Important Wake Timers Only". Before you change the value, take a photo of the current setting.
    3. Check device wake permissions — in Device Manager, check the power management tab of the network adapter, mouse and keyboard, and adjust the wake permission only when needed.
    4. Compare resume failures — if the screen stays black or the PC reboots, check Display, WHEA and Kernel-Power 41 from the same time together.
    5. Check the BIOS and drivers — if a sleep-related problem started after an update, compare the chipset, graphics and network drivers and the time of the BIOS change.
    Caution

    Do not conclude that the motherboard or power supply is faulty from event 1 or 42 alone. Separating the record of waking from sleep from the record of resume failure reduces unnecessary part replacement.

    Frequently asked questions

    • If there is a Power-Troubleshooter 1, did the PC turn on by itself? 1 is the event that records the cause of waking from sleep. The mouse, keyboard, network or a scheduled task can be the cause, so you need to check the value of Wake Source.
    • Does Kernel-Power 42 mean a power failure? 42 is the record that Windows entered sleep. Do not conclude it is a power failure; compare the actual sleep and resume times and the events of peripheral devices together.
