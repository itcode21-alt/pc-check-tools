    EVENT ID 129

    stornvme Event 129: NVMe Device Reset

    129 records that a request Storport sent to a storage device did not finish within the time limit and the path was reset. Microsoft's troubleshooting guidance also describes 129 as a timeout of a disk request, and the cause is not fixed to one SSD.

    storahci 129 means the same kind of reset happened on a SATA device, and stornvme 129 on an NVMe device. One or two may be a temporary delay; if it repeats together with freezes, compare the device, driver and power settings in that order.

    When it appears

    • A momentary freeze that releases during games, file copies or boot
    • Slow response for a while right after waking from sleep
    • stornvme 11 or Disk 153 and Ntfs records are left together at the same time

    Likely causes

    • SSD firmware or NVMe/chipset driver problems
    • Compatibility between the PCIe link state/power-management (sleep) settings and the SSD
    • Response delay caused by poor M.2 slot contact or overheating
    • A response delay or declining lifetime of the SSD itself

    What to check, in order

    1. Back up important files to another storage device first.
    2. Check the SSD state (SMART), temperature and firmware version with the maker's diagnostic tool.
    3. Compare whether stornvme 11, Disk 153 and Ntfs events are present at the same time.
    4. Check whether motherboard BIOS and chipset/NVMe driver updates are available.
    5. Change the power-management option to high performance and compare whether it reproduces.
    Caution

    Do not change the disk timeout (TimeOutValue) in the registry arbitrarily. Changing it without the maker's guidance can hide the cause.

    If freezes repeat, backup comes before checking.

    Frequently asked questions

    • Does 129 mean the SSD has failed? You cannot conclude that. Firmware, drivers, PCIe power management and heat can leave the same record. Judge it together with how often it repeats, the SMART state and stornvme 11 and Disk 153 records from the same time.
    • Does RaidPort0 mean I am using RAID? No. NVMe SSDs are also recorded under the same storage port name, and the number is a port number, not a disk number. Check which device it is from other records at the same time or the SSD maker's tool.
