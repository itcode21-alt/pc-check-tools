    EVENT ID 7032

    Service Control Manager Event 7032: Service Recovery Action Failed

    7032 records that the recovery action Windows tried after a service stopped has failed. Read the event where the service stopped once (7031, 7034) and the event where even the recovery then failed as two separate things.

    Microsoft's documentation tells you to check and change a service's recovery actions on the Recovery tab of its properties. The failure reason is written in the event body, so the direction of the check differs depending on whether the sentence says “already running” or “cannot be started”.

    When it appears

    • The service is not restarted automatically after it stopped
    • It follows right after a 7031 or 7034 record
    • The service's recovery setting is “Restart the service”

    Likely causes

    • The same service is already running when the restart is attempted, or a dependency is not ready
    • A problem with the service file, permissions or account
    • A program crash makes the service stop immediately again and again

    What to check, in order

    1. Write down the service name, the recovery action tried and the failure sentence in the event. Start with what is easy to undo so you can narrow the range without risk.
    2. On the Recovery tab of the service properties, check the actions for the first and second failure. Compare with the first check to narrow the cause candidates.
    3. Compare 7031, 7034 and Application Error 1000 at the same time. If it still reproduces at this step, move on to the next item.
    4. Start the service manually and see whether it starts. See whether the symptoms differ before and after the change.
    5. Update or repair the related program. If it is still unsolved after all this, review the next cause.
    Caution

    Changing the recovery action to “Restart the computer” can hide the cause, so it is not recommended unless it is a service you really need.

    Do not change the service account or permissions without knowing the cause.

    Frequently asked questions

    • How do I read a 7032 after a 7031? It means the service stopped (7031) and even the scheduled recovery action (such as restarting the service) failed. Check whether the service is still stopped and whether a manual start works.
    • Will changing the recovery settings fix it? The recovery action is only a response after a failure; it does not fix why the service stopped. Find the cause of the repeated stops (a crash, settings, a dependent service) first.
