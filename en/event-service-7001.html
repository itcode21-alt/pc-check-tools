    EVENT ID 7001

    Service Control Manager Event 7001: Dependency Service Failed

    7001 records that a service could not start because another service it depends on did not start. Check the name of the dependency shown in the description first.

    Starting the failed dependency by hand in the Services console shows whether only that service is broken or a whole chain is.

    How to read the description

    • Two service names appear in one sentence, and mixing up their roles means fixing the wrong service.
    • The name that comes first is the service that could not start. It shows the symptom, not the cause.
    • The name that comes after is the prerequisite service that actually failed. Investigate and act on that one.
    • So most of the work on a 7001 is really checking 7000 or 7023 for that second service.

    When it appears

    • At boot or when an app starts

    Likely causes

    • A prerequisite service failed
    • Damaged service configuration

    What to check, in order

    1. Identify the dependency in the description. The wording is “The X service depends on the Y service, which failed to start”. The service to fix is Y, not the X that is named prominently; restarting only X makes the same record repeat.
    2. Find the 7000/7023 record for Y from the same time: sort Event Viewer by time and look a few seconds before 7001. The real error text for Y is there and is the starting point.
    3. Follow the dependency tree to the end: in services.msc open Y's properties and the Dependencies tab. If Y depends on yet another service, check the very start of the chain; fixing a middle service does not help if the first one still fails.
    4. Repair the first service in the chain according to its own error text. Once it is healthy, the 7001 records that followed normally disappear without further action.
    Caution

    Do not edit the dependency registry entries at random.

    Frequently asked questions

    • How do I find out which dependency failed? The failed dependency's name is shown in the event description. Check its state and startup type in services.msc.
    • If 7000 and 7001 appear together, which is the cause? The service in the earlier 7000 is most likely where the failure started. Compare the times and the service names to confirm the order.
    • Where do I see what a service depends on? In services.msc, double-click the service and open the Dependencies tab: it lists what the service depends on and what depends on it.
